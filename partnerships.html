Question 1 · Part A · Warm up

Meera has ₹800 in pocket money this month. She has four possible expenses:

  • Exam stationery she needs this week (₹150)
  • College application fee due Friday, after which she loses her spot (₹250)
  • A new phone case she has been wanting (₹300)
  • A birthday gift for her best friend next week (₹200)

She cannot afford all four. What should she pay for first?

A · The phone case, since it is the one she wants most
B · The application fee, since missing that deadline cannot be reversed Correct
C · The birthday gift, so her friend is not upset
D · Split the ₹800 evenly across everything

Why B is right. When money is short, pay first for the cost that cannot be undone. Missing Friday’s application deadline loses the college spot for good.

IV · The reading

Two assessments. Neither is the score.

Students fill this cover first. Then we read the mark against board and household.

Assessment 1

Board

Why. One paper, one key, every board. We compare ICSE, CBSE, IGCSE, and IB. If board is missing we recover it from the school name. We never name or rank a school.

Assessment 2

Context

This is not part of your score. It helps us understand the results across different backgrounds. Your answers stay private.

How do you usually come to school?
Which of these do you personally own?
What is your family's monthly income?

For the socioeconomic reading. Not sure is allowed.

Why. Transport, parents’ work, area, devices, and income are how we read a mark against a household. None of it changes the score. Without it, a gap looks like ability. With it, we can see who was taught what, and from where.

North star

The paper shows the gap. The work is what we do with it.

Our north star is personalised, customised support toward financial independence for students in Grades 9 to 12 who were not handed that education — from every board, every kind of school, every walk of life.

Not a lecture for the already-advantaged. Not one booklet for everyone. Help that fits the student in front of us: how they borrow, how they save, how they are asked to decide, and what nobody has yet sat down and explained.

The first pilot is complete. More than a hundred students sat this paper in a short stretch of time. The instrument works. What we cannot do alone is reach the rooms where this would actually change a life.

If you can open those rooms — if your work is with young people who should not have to learn money the hard way — we should build the next round together.
