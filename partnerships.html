I · The sitting

30:00

The same thirty minutes for every student.

Grade 9 or Grade 12. English, Hindi, or Marathi — each version back-translated so the meaning holds. One paper, one key. The mark is never shown on the page.

  • The paper 27 questions · 25 marks
  • The languages English · Hindi · Marathi
  • The room Full screen. Leaving the tab counts as leaving the exam.
  • The close When the timer ends, answers go out on their own.

This is the specimen. Pick an option the way a student would. The note that follows is what the item is doing — the temptation, then the key.

III · The letter

The mark is not on the website. It arrives by letter.

  1. 0 min

    The student submits, or the timer closes the paper. A row is written. Nothing is shown on screen except that the sitting is recorded.

  2. ~60 min

    A letter goes out: the score, each part, what already holds, what to work on, and every miss — the pick in red, the key in green, and why.

Hello Riya

Thank you for sitting the paper. Your mark is not shown on the website. It is here, with a reading of what you already do well, what to work on, and every question you missed.

Your score

16.5 / 25
Part A · Warm up2 / 2.5Strong
Part B · Everyday6 / 7Strong
Part C · Read carefully7.5 / 12Work on this
Part D · Market awareness1 / 3.5Work on this

What you already do well

You can take a money idea and apply it to a real situation: inflation, compounding, insurance, and not putting everything on one tip.

Where you are weaker

Part C is written so every option sounds fair at first. Slow down, multiply, and ask what still holds if the next few years go badly. Learn the headline market facts. The “winner” changes when the clock is two years instead of thirty.

A miss, as the student sees it

Question 14 · Part C · Read carefully

A shop offers a phone on a “zero interest” EMI of ₹4,200 per month for 12 months. The same phone costs ₹45,000 if you pay in cash.

What is actually true about this offer?

A · It is genuinely free financing, since no interest is charged You picked this
B · The 12 EMIs add up to ₹50,400, which is ₹5,400 more than the cash price Correct
C · Paying by EMI is always cheaper, since your cash stays in the bank
D · Both options cost you the same amount in the end

Why A is not right. The poster says zero interest, but ₹4,200 × 12 = ₹50,400, which is ₹5,400 more than cash. That extra is the cost.

Why B is right. Always multiply. The EMI path costs ₹5,400 extra, whatever the poster says. That extra is often built into a higher EMI price.

A composite letter, using a made-up student and a typical miss. Real letters use the student’s own answers. Schools are never named.

IV · The reading

A mark without a household is only a number.

Before the first question, a short cover page. No name on the research sheet — an ID code, the board, and a handful of household facts. Together they let us read every result in context.

Cover page · not part of the score

  • How they come to school Walk · bus · family car · public transport
  • What their parents do Occupation, in their own words
  • Where they live Area only. No address.
  • What they own Phone, tablet, laptop — or none of these
  • Household income Bands, including “not sure”

Those answers form a socioeconomic reading, in the spirit of a Kuppuswamy index, without putting a name on the sheet. Board is recorded separately, and recovered from the school when it is missing, so ICSE, CBSE, IGCSE, and IB can be compared on the same paper.

The same mark. An ICSE household in the highest income band. On its own, 16.5 looks like a mid-paper sitting. Next to Student B, it is the beginning of a comparison the Index exists to make.
